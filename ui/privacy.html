Privacy

Draft for review. This policy has not yet been checked by a lawyer.

The short version

The program runs on your computer and keeps what it knows there, encrypted with your password. It is open source, and nothing is sent to the people who make it — no account on a server, no counting of copies, no reports sent anywhere. Information leaves your computer only when you use a feature that needs another service, and only to that service.

Each person on the computer has their own account. Another account — even on the same computer, in the same copy of the program — cannot open yours: it is locked with your password, and before you sign in the program shows only the names of the accounts, never which websites they look after.

What is kept on your computer

  • Your account: your name and the keys that lock your data (your password itself is never stored).
  • Your business profile and your websites’ details.
  • The keys you give it for other services.
  • What the agency works with: leads it finds (business names and their published contact details), drafts, approvals, website changes, reports and the record of every action.

All of it is encrypted with your password, and the program’s office answers only on your own computer.

The people in your leads

When the agency finds businesses to contact, you decide what is done with their details, so the law treats you as responsible for them. The program helps: it prefers a business’s published role address (such as info@), never writes to the same contact twice, follows each country’s rules as it knows them, and lets you delete what you no longer need. Stop contacting anyone who asks you to, and delete their details if they ask.

How long things are kept

As long as you keep them. Old records can be cleared from the program, and deleting your account removes its folder from your computer.

Children

The program is for businesses and is not meant for anyone under 18.
